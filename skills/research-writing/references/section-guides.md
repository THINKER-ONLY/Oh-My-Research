# Section Guides

Section names and order are selected by venue, field, artifact, and user intent. Treat the patterns below as rhetorical jobs, not mandatory headings.

Load the sections relevant to the current task. The section's rhetorical job never permits evidence drift.

## Abstract

State the question or problem, approach, evidence, result, and bounded implication. Keep the study design and scope that determine how the result can be read. Do not call an observational or uncontrolled change an effect.

## Introduction

Establish the motivating question, relevant context, and the manuscript's supported purpose. A problem-gap-insight progression can suit a contribution paper. For a replication or negative-result paper, state the prior claim and the replication question without manufacturing novelty. Distinguish motivation from evidence. List only contributions actually present in the materials; attach any quantitative teaser to its design and scope.

## Related Work

Group work by the question it addresses, then state the paper's relation or difference. Verify author, year, venue, and claim-source fit before citing. If the requested citation or result cannot be verified, omit the asserted comparison and set `citation_status` to `pending-verification`; never manufacture a plausible reference.

## Methods

Make the work reproducible: inputs, selection rules, procedure, model or system version, prompts or settings, measures, and analysis. Separate planned protocol from completed work. Do not imply controls, randomization, blinding, or validation that did not occur.

## Experiments and Results

Name the unit of analysis, comparison, sample or corpus, measure, and result. Preserve exact values, units, denominators, and direction. Present descriptive changes as descriptive unless the design and analysis justify stronger inference. Point each table or figure to the claim it supports.

## Discussion

Interpret only within the evidence's scope. Explain what the result suggests, enumerate conclusion-changing limits, and distinguish mechanisms or hypotheses from demonstrated findings. Do not use a limitation paragraph to rescue an overstrong earlier claim; fix the claim itself.

## Conclusion

Restate the supported finding, synthesis, artifact, or contribution and its bounded implication without introducing new evidence. Retain material limits in a compact form and name the next verification or evaluation needed.

## Paragraph Check

For each paragraph, identify: (1) its job, (2) one controlling claim, (3) evidence or qualification, and (4) a link to the next paragraph. Split paragraphs that make independently checkable claims with different evidence statuses.
